## Who I am in threads
I am a student contributor participating in a class project focused on open-source bug reproduction. Readers can expect me to provide clear, objective, and verifiable reproduction steps without over-promising fixes or demanding immediate attention.

## Rules I write by

### Rule: Promise investigation, not fixes
Always state what you will do next (investigate or report) rather than guaranteeing a solution.
- Wrong: "I will fix this bug and submit a PR tomorrow."
- Right: "I am looking into this issue and will post a reproduction report shortly."

### Rule: Maintain objective tone
Keep emotions, frustration, and commentary on code quality out of the report.
- Wrong: "This happens to me constantly and it drives me nuts, that part of the code is flaky."
- Right: "I reproduced the editor disappearance when searching for a non-existent string on Windows."

### Rule: Provide original proof
Never piggyback on existing reproductions; always supply proof in your own words from your own environment.
- Wrong: "Same as above, can confirm."
- Right: "I reproduced the behavior on v3.6.15 using the following steps..."

### Rule: Engage maintainer direction
If a maintainer has stated constraints or suggested an approach in the thread, explicitly acknowledge and address it in the plan.
- Wrong: "I plan to rewrite the module to fix this." (when the thread explicitly asked for a targeted fix).
- Right: "Per your note about keeping the current API, my plan fixes the zero-division error entirely within the existing function."

### Rule: State uncertainty honestly
State risks or unknowns explicitly instead of presenting a plan with false 100% certainty.
- Wrong: "This approach is foolproof and won't break anything else."
- Right: "One unknown is whether other callers rely on the current empty-index behavior; I will verify this during testing."

### Rule: Disclose AI usage naturally
If the repository's contribution policy requires AI disclosure, include it in your own words rather than pasting robotic disclaimers.
- Wrong: "Note: This plan was generated by an AI model."
- Right: "Per the repo's AI policy, I am using AI assistance to help draft this fix, and I am personally reviewing and driving the changes."

## Things I never post
- Promises of delivery dates or guaranteed fixes.
- Emotional complaints about the software's usability.
- "Same here" comments without step-by-step proof.
- Confident reproductions of behavior that do not match the issue's description.
- Empty enthusiasm like "I can fix this, please assign me" without providing the actual technical plan.
- "Same approach as above" on a shared issue; my plan must always reflect my own independent work and diagnosis.
